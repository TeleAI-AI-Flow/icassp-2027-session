ICASSP 2027 Special Session:
Generative Transmission for Distributed Intelligent Signal Processing based on Large Foundation Models and Beneficial Noise Models
16 - 21 May 2027, Toronto, Canada

About

Recent advances in large foundation models (LFMs) are driving a paradigm shift in signal processing and communications: from transmitting data bits to transmitting and understanding intelligence. However, the sheer scale of LFMs, limited edge resources, and constrained network bandwidth make ubiquitous intelligent services difficult under conventional communication frameworks.

This special session is motivated by AI Flow, where device-edge-cloud networks serve as intelligent infrastructures for adaptive cooperation among models, agents, and users. In distributed intelligence systems, multi-agent collaboration enables agents to share compact long-term experiences and retrieve role-relevant information, reducing redundant context transmission while improving cooperative reasoning under heterogeneous edge-cloud environments.

A key focus of the session is generative transmission, which integrates communication-oriented reconstruction and understanding. Instead of sending raw data, transmitters deliver compact semantic representations, while generative models at the receiver reconstruct task-relevant content and support downstream reasoning. To make such a reconstruction-understanding mechanism scalable across heterogeneous devices, familial models of different scales with shared representations enable hierarchical understanding and cooperative inference between lightweight edge models and powerful foundation models.

As generative transmission relies on generative models based on noise scheduling (e.g., diffusion models and probability flow models), the session also emphasizes beneficial noise models, and in particular positive-incentive noise, a scientific framework that rethinks random noise as a beneficial factor for multimodal signal processing. By combining generative transmission, familial-model cooperation, collaborative multi-agent systems, and positive-incentive noise, this session aims to explore new foundations for distributed intelligent signal processing in future 6G.

The special session will be held at ICASSP 2027, the 52nd IEEE International Conference on Acoustics, Speech, and Signal Processing, May 16-21, 2027, Toronto, Canada.

Call for Papers

Recent advances in large foundation models (LFMs) are driving a paradigm shift in signal processing and communications: from transmitting data bits to transmitting and understanding intelligence. This special session at ICASSP 2027 provides a focused forum on how intelligence can be generated, transmitted, reconstructed, understood, and collaboratively refined under bandwidth and computation constraints. Building on AI Flow, it brings together researchers from communications, signal processing, machine learning, and distributed AI to establish new foundations for distributed intelligent signal processing in future 6G. Papers submitted to this session follow the standard ICASSP 2027 submission and review process.

Scope and Topics of Interests

How intelligence can be generated, transmitted, reconstructed, understood, and collaboratively refined under bandwidth and computation constraints is not yet systematically covered by regular conference submissions. This session welcomes original contributions on generative transmission, familial-model cooperation, multi-agent collaboration, and beneficial noise for distributed intelligent signal processing. Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Generative transmission and generative compression of compact semantic representations
  • Receiver-side generative reconstruction and communication-oriented understanding
  • Familial-model assisted hierarchical inference across device-edge-cloud networks
  • Multi-agent collaboration with compact long-term experience sharing and role-aware retrieval
  • Beneficial noise and positive-incentive noise modeling for multimodal signal processing
  • Robust multimodal transmission under bandwidth and computation constraints
  • Semantic distortion and noise-benefit tradeoffs in generative transmission
  • Efficient cooperation among heterogeneous foundation-model agents
